# saunas.mx: SEO, topic architecture, keyword research and GEO operating system

## Executive recommendation

Build `saunas.mx` as a Spanish-first authority and lead-generation site about **sauna, heat exposure, contrast therapy, recovery and practical wellness in Mexico**.

Use sleep, performance, healthy aging, body composition and sexual vitality as adjacent topic clusters, but do not position the site as a generic “anti-aging” or “sexual-performance” publisher. Those themes are broad, highly competitive and medically sensitive. They should support the sauna thesis, not replace it.

The commercial path should be:

`wellness question → evidence-based explanation → safe use guide → sauna/contrast decision guide → supplier consultation or quote`

Do not publish search-engine-first content at scale. Google’s current guidance says there are no special technical requirements or special schema types for AI Overviews or AI Mode; normal crawlability, indexability, helpful people-first content, clear authorship, visible evidence and accurate structured data remain the foundation. [Google: AI features and your website](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) [Google: helpful, reliable, people-first content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)

This document is both the strategy and the implementation brief for Claude Code.

## 2. Strategic positioning

### Recommended positioning statement

> `saunas.mx` ayuda a las personas en México a entender, usar y comprar saunas y experiencias de contraste con información clara, práctica y basada en evidencia.

### Editorial promise

Every page should help the reader do at least one of these things:

1. Understand what sauna or contrast therapy is.
2. Decide whether a claim is supported, uncertain or exaggerated.
3. Use heat or cold more safely.
4. Choose a sauna type, size, location or supplier.
5. Take a concrete next step toward a purchase or consultation.

### Audience segments

1. **The curious beginner** — searches for benefits, risks and how to start.
2. **The recovery-focused exerciser** — wants better recovery, sleep and performance.
3. **The wellness buyer** — is comparing infrared, traditional, steam and contrast systems.
4. **The home-improvement buyer** — needs dimensions, electricity, installation, maintenance and cost.
5. **The healthy-aging reader** — interested in long-term function, cardiovascular health and longevity, but vulnerable to exaggerated claims.
6. **The sensitive sexual-health reader** — searching for performance-related answers; route responsibly to evidence and professional care, not miracle claims.

### Brand voice

- Spanish from Mexico; clear, adult, calm and practical.
- Explain the mechanism without pretending certainty.
- Prefer “puede”, “se ha asociado con”, “la evidencia es limitada” and “no sustituye atención médica” when appropriate.
- Avoid biohacking hype, bro-science, miracle language and shame-based selling.
- Make the product feel desirable through ritual, comfort, design, recovery and home experience—not unsupported medical promises.

## 5. Keyword research process using DataForSEO

Do not make decisions from a single search-volume number. Build a keyword-to-intent dataset.

### DataForSEO workflow

1. Start with the seed keywords in this document.
2. Run Google keyword data for `Mexico` and Spanish-language query variants.
3. If a language parameter causes null or incomplete volume, keep the Spanish phrase itself and use the Mexico location; DataForSEO documents that language fields can return null and that location can be supplied by name or code. [DataForSEO Google search volume documentation](https://docs.dataforseo.com/v3/keywords_data/google/search_volume/live/)
4. Use batches of up to 700 keywords per request; keep a dated raw export.
5. Expand with:
   - Keywords for keywords.
   - Keywords for site after the first version is indexed.
   - SERP competitor and ranked-keyword data.
   - Related searches and People Also Ask questions.
   - Search intent classification.
   - Historical trend data where available.
6. Run separate datasets for:
   - Mexico Spanish.
   - Mexico with English-language phrases such as “cold plunge”, “infrared sauna” and “wellness”.
   - English-speaking markets only after Spanish data supports a separate expansion.
7. De-duplicate accents, singular/plural variants and obvious close variants, but preserve colloquial queries when the SERP intent differs.
8. Cluster by actual SERP overlap, not just by semantic similarity.
9. Assign one primary URL per intent cluster. Do not publish three pages for “sauna y sueño” unless the queries clearly represent different jobs.

## 6. Content model and page templates

### Article template

Every article should include, in visible HTML:

1. Clear title and one-sentence answer to the main question.
2. `Resumen rápido` with 3–5 bullets.
3. Who the article is for and what it does not cover.
4. Definition or context.
5. Evidence section:
   - What research suggests.
   - What remains uncertain.
   - Study type and limitations.
6. Practical guidance, clearly labeled as general information.
7. Safety and who should consult a clinician.
8. FAQ with real user questions; no invented questions solely for schema.
9. Related articles and one relevant commercial next step.
10. Sources with direct links.
11. Author, reviewer, publication date and last reviewed date.
12. Clear update history when material facts change.

Place this near the top of each article:

Respuesta corta: [direct answer in 40–80 words]

## 7. Evidence and health-claim governance

This is a wellness site that will publish health-adjacent information. Treat the content as YMYL.

### Evidence labels

- **Level A:** clinical guideline, public-health agency, systematic review or high-quality meta-analysis.
- **Level B:** controlled trial or multiple consistent observational studies.
- **Level C:** single study, small study or indirect evidence.
- **Level D:** expert opinion, mechanism or user experience; never present as proof.
- **PENDIENTE:** claim needs a source or qualified review before publication.

### Approved claim language

- “La evidencia disponible sugiere…”
- “Se ha observado una asociación…”
- “Los resultados son preliminares…”
- “Esto no demuestra que la sauna cause…”
- “La respuesta puede variar…”
- “No sustituye una evaluación médica…”

### Prohibited or hold-for-review claims

Do not publish these as facts without unusually strong, directly relevant evidence and qualified review:

- Sauna detoxifies the body.
- Sauna burns meaningful body fat or replaces exercise.
- Sauna cures insomnia.
- Sauna treats erectile dysfunction, infertility or low testosterone.
- Sauna prevents cancer, dementia or cardiovascular disease.
- Sauna extends lifespan.
- Cold plunges “boost immunity” in a clinically meaningful way.
- A specific temperature/time protocol is universally safe.
- A product is “medical grade”, “therapeutic” or “clinically proven” without documentation.

### Safety baseline

Sauna safety content should mention hydration, gradual cooling, alcohol/impairing substances, stopping when unwell and clinician consultation for relevant conditions. Harvard Health notes that people with uncontrolled blood pressure or heart disease should consult their doctors and advises avoiding alcohol, limiting sessions and cooling down gradually. [Harvard Health: sauna safety](https://www.health.harvard.edu/healthy-aging-and-longevity/saunas-and-your-health)

Cold-water content must not normalize sudden extreme exposure. Cold-water immersion can trigger cold-shock responses involving gasping, hyperventilation, increased cardiac workload and arrhythmia risk; the first minute and the person’s medical context matter. [PubMed: cold-shock response review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38211547/) [CDC Yellow Book: cold-water immersion](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK620915/)

### Source hierarchy

Prefer:

1. Government and public-health agencies.
2. Professional medical organizations.
3. Systematic reviews and meta-analyses.
4. Peer-reviewed primary research.
5. Major academic medical centers.
6. Expert interviews with named credentials.

Do not use influencers, retailer blogs, affiliate sites or anonymous summaries as the sole support for a health claim.

### Mexico-specific compliance checkpoint

Before making therapeutic, preventive, rehabilitative, medical-device, supplement or treatment claims, obtain Mexico-specific legal/compliance review. COFEPRIS states that advertising involving health services and certain products is subject to sanitary regulation and warns against unsupported therapeutic, preventive or rehabilitative qualities. [COFEPRIS: publicidad sanitaria](https://www.gob.mx/cofepris/acciones-y-programas/autorizacion-publicitaria) [COFEPRIS: publicidad irregular en internet](https://www.gob.mx/cofepris/es/articulos/cofepris-alerta-a-la-poblacion-sobre-publicidad-irregular-en-sitios-de-internet)

## 8. GEO and AI-discovery specification

### Core principle

There is no reliable “AI ranking hack”. Optimize for a page that is easy for a person, crawler and answer engine to understand, quote and verify.

### Required technical behavior

- Server-render important article text in the initial HTML.
- Do not hide the answer, sources, author, safety notes or product facts behind client-only interactions.
- Allow indexing for public editorial and commercial pages.
- Use one canonical URL per page.
- Use descriptive titles, meta descriptions and headings.
- Use clean internal links from category pages, pillar pages and related articles.
- Generate XML sitemaps with accurate `lastmod` values.
- Keep `robots.txt` accessible and reviewed after deployment.
- Make `sitemap.xml` discoverable from `robots.txt` and Search Console/Bing Webmaster Tools.
- Use `hreflang` only if a genuine English version exists and is materially adapted, not machine-translated duplication.
- Keep page speed, mobile usability, accessibility and image alt text strong.
- Use descriptive ARIA labels for interactive forms, selectors and comparison tools.

### Crawler policy

If the business wants content discoverable in AI search:

- Do not block `Googlebot`.
- Do not block `Bingbot`.
- Do not block `OAI-SearchBot` from public pages intended to appear in ChatGPT search.
- Do not block `PerplexityBot` from public pages intended to be indexed there.
- Decide separately whether `GPTBot` should be allowed for model training; this is an ownership and policy decision, not an SEO requirement.

OpenAI’s publisher guidance says public websites can appear in ChatGPT search and that allowing `OAI-SearchBot` supports inclusion in summaries and snippets; it also notes that ChatGPT referrals carry `utm_source=chatgpt.com`. [OpenAI: Publishers and Developers FAQ](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/12627856-publishers-and-developers-faq)

Perplexity says `PerplexityBot` follows `robots.txt`. [Perplexity: robots.txt](https://www.perplexity.ai/help-center/en/articles/10354969-how-does-perplexity-follow-robots-txt)

Bing recommends crawlability, accurate sitemaps and real `lastmod` dates for AI-powered discovery and now provides AI citation/grounding-query insights in Bing Webmaster Tools. [Bing: AI Performance](https://blogs.bing.com/webmaster/February-2026/Introducing-AI-Performance-in-Bing-Webmaster-Tools-Public-Preview) [Bing: sitemaps in AI-powered search](https://blogs.bing.com/webmaster/July-2025/Keeping-Content-Discoverable-with-Sitemaps-in-AI-Powered-Search)

### Structured data

Use JSON-LD where it accurately describes visible content:

- `Organization` or `OnlineBusiness` for the site entity.
- `WebSite` and `SearchAction` if an internal site search exists.
- `BreadcrumbList` for hierarchical pages.
- `Article` or `BlogPosting` for editorial content.
- `Person` for real authors and reviewers.
- `Product`, `Offer` and `AggregateRating` only where all fields are real, visible and verified.
- `FAQPage` only when the questions and answers are visible and eligible under current search guidance; never use schema as a hidden content layer.

Google says structured data should describe the page’s visible content, and recommends complete, accurate JSON-LD where practical. [Google: structured data](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/intro-structured-data)

Do not create `llms.txt`, hidden keyword blocks, fake reviews, fake authors or unsupported “AI schema” as a substitute for good content. Google explicitly says there are no special AI text files or special schema required for AI Overviews or AI Mode. [Google: AI features and your website](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)

### Article structure optimized for retrieval and citation

Each article should make these entities explicit:

- Main question.
- Short answer.
- Definitions.
- Audience and context.
- Claims and evidence.
- Limitations.
- Safety conditions.
- Practical next step.
- Author/reviewer.
- Dates.
- Sources.
- Related pages.

Use short paragraphs, descriptive H2/H3 headings, tables where comparisons are real, and question-shaped headings that reflect user language. Do not write choppy prose solely to force extractable sentences.

### Measurement

Track:

- Google Search Console impressions, clicks, queries, CTR and indexed pages.
- GA4 qualified lead events by landing page and topic cluster.
- ChatGPT referral traffic, including `utm_source=chatgpt.com`.
- Bing Webmaster Tools AI citations, cited pages and grounding-query phrases when available.
- Monthly manual test queries in Google AI features, Bing/Copilot, ChatGPT search and Perplexity.
- Citation rate: cited answers / tested queries.
- Recommendation rate: answers that mention the brand or a relevant page.
- Qualified traffic rate: sessions that view a commercial guide, comparison, supplier page or quote form.
- Lead rate by cluster.

Never promise a universal ranking position or guaranteed AI citations.
